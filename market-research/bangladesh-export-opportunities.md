# Bangladesh's Best Export Opportunities (Beyond RMG)
*Research date: July 2026*

## Why this matters right now

Ready-made garments (knitwear + woven) still make up **~82% of Bangladesh's exports**, and the country faces a hard deadline: **formal LDC graduation in November 2026**, with the EU's Everything But Arms (EBA) duty-free access only guaranteed through a **three-year transition to November 2029**. After that, standard GSP tariffs (~9.6% on most garments) kick in unless Bangladesh secures GSP+ (which requires the labour/governance/environmental commitments it's currently working through — it has already ratified ILO Conventions 155, 187, and 190 as a first step). WTO estimates put **up to $8B/year (~14% of total exports) at risk** once preferences lapse. That's the backdrop for why diversification isn't optional — it's a live deadline, not a long-term nice-to-have.

Ranked below by a mix of current growth rate, documented market size, and how exposed each sector already is to that 2026–2029 tariff cliff.

## Ranked opportunities

### 1. Leather footwear & finished leather goods — strongest momentum
- Leather footwear exports up **28.96% YoY** (Jul 2024–May 2025) to $620M; overall footwear sector hit **$1.5B in 2024 at 45% YoY growth**; Bangladesh is now the 18th-largest footwear exporter globally, with government/industry targeting **$5B within 5 years**.
- Global buyers are actively shifting sourcing away from China into Bangladesh — this is a live "China+1" beneficiary, not a speculative bet.
- **The actual opportunity is narrower than "leather" as a category**: Bangladesh still exports too much raw/semi-processed (wet-blue) hide instead of finished goods, leaving the highest-margin step to buyers abroad. The real opportunity is finished leather footwear, bags, and wallets — not tanning/hide export.

### 2. Pharmaceuticals — generics + API, with a closing window
- Export earnings reached **$213M in FY25** (+3.74% YoY, more than double FY18's $103M), now reaching **166 countries**, backed by a growing base of US FDA / UK MHRA / EU GMP-certified plants.
- Bangladesh's TRIPS waiver (letting it manufacture patented generics royalty-free as an LDC) also lapses around the 2026 graduation — so there's a real time-boxed advantage to lock in generic-drug export contracts and API (active pharmaceutical ingredient) capacity now, before both the tariff and IP protections shift.
- Sweet spot: generics for African and South/Southeast Asian markets that don't require the most stringent regulatory dossiers, plus API import-substitution manufacturing rather than finished-formulation-only exports.

### 3. Frozen shrimp & processed seafood — riding a real demand spike
- Seafood export value rose **19.3% YoY to $388.7M**, explicitly driven by **stronger demand from India and China** (23,238 MT of frozen shrimp, +21.5% by volume) — this is happening right now, not a forecast.
- Caveat: raw shrimp production itself is structurally declining (projected ~16,618 MT in FY26-27, versus 54,891 MT in FY10-11) due to farming/environmental constraints — so the opportunity isn't "export more raw shrimp," it's moving up the value chain into **value-added, ready-to-cook/processed frozen seafood** for the India/China corridor, where the processed-food segment overall is growing ~16.6%/year even as raw commodity volumes shrink.

### 4. Jute diversified products (JDP) — a genuine sustainability-trend beneficiary
- Bangladesh is the **world's #1 exporter of jute and jute products** (and #2 producer). Jute bag exports are growing **~20%/year**, and the global jute bag market alone is projected at **$3.2B by 2026** with eco-friendly jute products broadly seeing a ~21.3% CAGR.
- This directly overlaps with the plastic-bag-replacement trend already showing up in India's consumer market (see the earlier dropshipping research — jute/eco bags are a named India trend). Bangladesh is positioned to supply that demand at the industrial/wholesale level, not just retail.
- The specific product lane is **JDP** (~300 distinct product lines: home textiles, geo-textiles, fashion accessories, packaging) rather than raw jute fiber/yarn, where margins are thin. The government-backed **Sonali Bag** (a 100%-biodegradable jute-based bioplastic, with a ৳100-crore investment targeting 1M bags/day capacity) is the highest-profile example of where this is headed — a genuine plastic-replacement export product, not just a "green" bag.

### 5. Light engineering — bicycle parts and machine components
- The light engineering sector grew at a **22–28% CAGR (2017–2022)**, reaching **$795.6M in exports by 2022**. Bicycles are the single largest item in that sector: exports went from $45.2M (2004) to **$192.6M (2022)** — a ~4x increase in under two decades.
- Government policy explicitly targets a 40%-higher GDP contribution from this sector by 2027, making it one of the more actively state-supported diversification bets.
- Opportunity is specifically **bicycle components/parts and light machinery parts** as a China+1 supply-chain play, not finished consumer bicycles competing head-on with established brands.

### 6. IT/ITES and software export services — big local sector, tiny export share (the real gap)
- Bangladesh's domestic ICT *market* is ~$9.44B (growing 6.25% CAGR) and there are **4,500+ IT/ITES firms employing 750,000+ people**, plus **over 1 million active freelancers** exporting to 80+ countries. But actual **export earnings from computer services were only $724.6M in FY25** — badly missing the government's own $5B target.
- That gap between domestic capacity and actual export revenue is the opportunity: Bangladesh already has the talent base (proven by the freelancer numbers) but is badly under-monetizing it as formal exports, largely due to payment/remittance friction and lack of enterprise-level (vs. gig-level) contracts. Niche outsourced services — accounting/bookkeeping BPO, medical coding, custom software development, and QA/testing for US/UK/Australian SMBs — are the underexploited lane, as opposed to competing on general-purpose freelance marketplaces where Bangladesh is already saturated.

### 7. Ceramics & home textiles — real but smaller, thinner data
- Ceramic tiles and sanitary ware are established export lines but represent a much smaller segment than the above, with limited hard 2026 figures publicly available — worth tracking but not sizeable enough yet to lead a diversification strategy.
- Home textiles (bedsheets, towels, terry products) already sit at ~3.3% of the garment-adjacent export basket — a natural adjacency to existing RMG expertise/supply chains rather than a new sector, lower risk but also lower upside than the six above.

## The strategic read

- **Most exposed to the LDC deadline**: RMG and leather (both EBA-dependent) — diversifying *within* leather toward finished goods is a hedge against the same tariff risk it's trying to escape, so it only works if paired with quality/design upgrading, not volume growth alone.
- **Best "growth is already happening, just ride it" picks**: leather footwear and frozen seafood (India/China demand) — both have real, current double-digit YoY growth already in motion.
- **Best "underexploited capacity" pick**: IT/ITES — the gap between a $9B+ domestic tech sector and <$1B in actual exports is the widest capability-vs-monetization mismatch in this list.
- **Best "aligned with a global consumer trend" pick**: jute diversified products — it's the one sector where Bangladesh's traditional strength (jute) directly intersects a live global sustainability/plastic-replacement trend rather than just competing on cost.
- **Time-boxed opportunity**: pharmaceuticals — the TRIPS waiver and LDC-linked advantages make the next 2–3 years a use-it-or-lose-it window for locking in generic-drug and API export relationships before the regulatory environment shifts.

Sources: World Bank, WTO, The Business Standard, The Daily Star, East Asia Forum, LightCastle Partners, SeafoodSource, Mordor Intelligence, and sector-specific trade/industry reports (see search citations in session).
